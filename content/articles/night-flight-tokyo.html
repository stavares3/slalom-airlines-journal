Night flight to Tokyo

Watch the film

SL 100 leaves Seattle for Tokyo in the early evening and lands the following evening, local time, having flown through one entire night that the calendar refuses to acknowledge. Ten and a half hours, most of them over open water. Here is that night, hour by hour, filmed from the cabin and written down from the jump seat of memory.

Hour one belongs to the descent of the sun. We climb out over the Sound while the Olympics go violet, and by the time the seatbelt chime relaxes, the last light is a long orange line behind the winglet. Dinner arrives while the sky still holds color. There is a particular pleasure in eating a proper meal at thirty six thousand feet while the day ends in slow motion outside your window.

Hours two and three are the settling. Trays cleared, shades half down, the cabin lighting eased to amber and then to almost nothing. The film crowd surrenders first, then the readers. By hour three the cabin is a row of small private glows, and the engines have become the sound the silence is made of.

Hour five is the middle of everything. The moving map shows an airplane alone on a blue circle, equidistant from all land, and somewhere in this hour we cross the line where the date quietly gives way. Walk to the galley if you are awake. The crew keep a tray of things worth being awake for, and the conversation at the aft door at 3 a.m. Pacific is one of aviation's small secret rooms.

Hour seven brings the false dawn, a gray suggestion at the horizon that the airplane is chasing west and losing. Hour eight, the real one, arrives fast and floods the crew rest curtain in gold. Watch the wing in this light. The film above lingers on it, because no one on the crew has ever gotten tired of it.

Hour ten is Honshu, the coastline arriving as a thin geometry of lights coming on for the evening we are landing into. Descent over the rice country, the bay, the turn. Wheels down at Narita as the airport lamps warm up, one night older and one day ahead, depending on how you choose to count.

The short film above was shot across this route by our own crews. Press play when you are somewhere you can watch a wing think for a moment.
