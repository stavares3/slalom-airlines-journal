Our fare classes, honestly

Airline fare pages are written to sell the top of the range. This one is written to help you buy correctly, which sometimes means buying less. Slalom Airlines sells four cabins, and each one is the right answer to a different question. Here is what each actually gets you, and the honest rule of thumb for when it earns its price.

Economy is the airline. Most of our seats, most of our flights, most of our story. You get a standard seat, one carry-on, and an aircraft that is Wi-Fi ready nose to tail. What you also get, and what the fare pages undersell, is the same crew, the same on-time performance, and the same window view as every other cabin on the airplane. The honest rule: under three hours, Economy is the correct answer for almost everyone, and spending more is buying jewelry, not transportation.

Premium Select is the one most travelers should study. Extra legroom, priority boarding, two checked bags, and a dining service that comes off real plates. The bags alone change the math for families and for anyone moving between seasons of their life. The honest rule: between three and six hours, or any time you are checking two bags anyway, Premium Select usually costs less than the sum of its parts bought separately.

Summit Business is where the product changes kind rather than degree. The seat becomes a lie-flat suite, the lounge opens before the flight, the menu is chef curated, and the arrival fast track returns an hour of your first day at the destination. The honest rule: on a daytime flight it is a luxury, and on an overnight flight longer than six hours it is closer to a strategy. If you land at eight in the morning and negotiate at eleven, the suite is not indulgence. It is preparation.

First Ascent is small, deliberate, and not on every route. A private cabin, a chauffeur transfer at both ends, a sommelier who walks the aisle like it is a cellar, and a spa amenity kit that has caused at least one missed hotel checkout. The honest rule: nobody needs First Ascent. It exists for the flights you have decided should be part of the trip rather than the cost of it, and on those flights it delivers completely.

One closing note on how to buy. Prices move, cabins clear upgrade space at predictable times, and the difference between cabins is smallest when booked early. Buy the cabin your flight length argues for, book it sooner than feels natural, and spend whatever you saved at the destination, which is the point of all of this anyway.
