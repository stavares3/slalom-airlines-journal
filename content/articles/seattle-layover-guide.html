Five hours in Seattle, done well

A five hour layover at our home airport is not a sentence to be served in a gate area. It is enough time to see the city, eat something you will describe to strangers later, and still walk back onto your connection unhurried. This is the routine our own Seattle crews recommend, timed conservatively and tested more often than any of them will admit on the record.

The math first, because the math is what makes the rest possible. Link light rail runs from the airport station to downtown in 38 minutes, with trains every 8 to 10 minutes through the day. Budget 45 minutes back, 30 minutes for security at peak, and 15 minutes to your gate. That reserves two hours of margin from a five hour layover and leaves you roughly two full hours in the city itself. If your inbound flight is running late, the plan shrinks gracefully: everything below is in walking distance of one station.

Ride to Westlake and walk downhill to Pike Place Market. Go early if you can. The market at nine in the morning belongs to the people who work it, and the first hour of trading is the best theater in the city, fishmongers included. Coffee is a personal matter in Seattle and we will not legislate it, but the quiet counter tucked behind the market's main arcade pours a cup worth the detour, and the original tourist line up the street is worth seeing even if you do not join it.

Eat one thing, properly. The oyster bar at the market's north end shucks whatever came off the boats that morning, and a half dozen with a view of the Sound takes twenty minutes you will remember longer than most vacations. If the water is not your style, the sandwich counter on Post Alley cures its own pastrami and understands that you have a flight to catch.

Then give the last thirty minutes to the waterfront. Walk the new promenade south toward the ferry terminal and watch the boats trade places against the Olympics. This is the view our route map grows out from, and it explains the city faster than any museum could.

Back at the airport, through security, there is one move left. The observation walkway above the central terminal faces the runways west toward the mountains, and at sunset it is the best free show in any airport we serve. Five hours, spent, none of them in a gate area chair. Welcome to our home town.
